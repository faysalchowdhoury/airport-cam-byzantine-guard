\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
International airports cannot treat a compromised camera the way an integrity-first site such as a nuclear plant would.
Taking cameras offline on suspicion creates blind zones over dense passenger and airside traffic, so the security
objective is \emph{availability-first}: keep every critical zone watched by at least one camera whose stream is live and
untampered. We formalize this with a Site-Class-Aware Security Priority Vector (SCSPV) and model $n$ surveillance cameras,
$k$ of which are Byzantine (through Wi-Fi deauthentication, RTSP saturation, or drone-relayed stream injection), as a
bounded-degree visibility graph. We prove a Compression-by-Contradiction theorem. A colluding adversary cannot compress a
fabricated trajectory into its own $k$ views while staying consistent with the healthy views that share the epipolar
overlap ($p^\top E p'=0$). Detection therefore needs per-zone redundancy $k{+}1$ for ground-plane targets and $k{+}2$ for
off-plane targets, and masking needs $2k{+}1$ and $2k{+}2$. Every tight instance has an explicit pair of
indistinguishable worlds, and an exhaustive check of \TheoryCases{} cases has \TheoryMismatch{} mismatches. Because
cameras covering a common zone form a clique, the bound also caps tolerable collusion at
$k\le\lfloor\Delta/2\rfloor$ for a visibility graph of maximum degree $\Delta$. On the constructive side, a causal
spatio-temporal graph neural network (ST-GNN) fuses cross-view epipolar residuals, frame-jitter telemetry and an
untrusted ADS-B/Remote-ID prior whose influence is provably bounded. Its risk scores drive a mixed-integer linear program
(MILP) that hands analytics capacity off to healthy overlapping cameras. All experiments use real data: the calibrated
seven-camera WILDTRACK benchmark, live OpenSky ADS-B state vectors, and OpenStreetMap aerodrome geometry. Across five
seeds and $k/n\in[0.14,0.43]$, the attestor reaches a frame AUROC of \OursAUROC{} and \OursBlind{} coverage-weighted
blind minutes per hour, against \BestBaseBlind{} for the best baseline. The geometric core of the attestor runs in
\LatAttestPninetyfive{}\,ms (p95) per frame on one CPU thread.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{irport} video surveillance is a cyber-physical system built on commodity IP cameras, wireless backhaul
and RTSP streaming, and each of these can be degraded without touching the camera. Wi-Fi deauthentication frames force
bursty loss~\cite{bellardo2003deauth}, flooding an RTSP server saturates its sessions, and a drone hovering near a mast
can relay or replay a camera's own footage. A compromised edge device can also emit fabricated analytics metadata while
its pixels look normal~\cite{costin2016iotcam}. Airports add a second channel of untrusted information. ADS-B and the
FAA Remote~ID rule~\cite{faa2021remoteid} broadcast positions that anyone can receive, and anyone can spoof
them~\cite{strohmeier2015security}.

The defensive goal depends on the site class. An integrity-first site (zero-passenger nuclear facility, substation)
prefers to fail closed, which means quarantining a doubtful camera and accepting the blind spot. An airport cannot
afford that. Every quarantined camera over an apron, gate frontage or check-in hall is a blind zone over thousands of
people per hour, and every false alarm triggers an operator dispatch that disrupts the terminal. We make this explicit
with the \emph{Site-Class-Aware Security Priority Vector} (SCSPV), which weights availability, integrity and disruption
per site class and drives every operating decision in the controller.

This paper asks a question that comes before any detector design. How much cross-camera redundancy is
\emph{information-theoretically} necessary to attest a zone when up to $k$ cameras collude? It then asks how to spend a
limited analytics budget so that coverage-weighted blind time stays low. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{SCSPV and bounded-degree visibility model} (Section~\ref{sec:model}): cameras form a visibility graph
whose edges are shared fields of view. Its maximum degree $\Delta$ bounds the redundancy any zone can have.
\item \textbf{Compression-by-Contradiction theorem} (Section~\ref{sec:theory}): tight detection and masking bounds
$m(n,k)$ for ground-plane and off-plane targets. The off-plane bounds cost one extra witness because the adversary can
exploit epipolar degeneracy. We also give a probabilistic extension with imperfect honest witnesses and a
bounded-degree corollary $k\le\lfloor\Delta/2\rfloor$.
\item \textbf{Constructive attestor} (Section~\ref{sec:method}): a causal ST-GNN over the visibility graph, with no
physics-informed loss. It uses edge features built from epipolar and homography residuals and gates the ADS-B prior so
the prior's influence on the logit is provably bounded. An availability-weighted MILP then reallocates analytics slots.
\item \textbf{Real-data evaluation} (Sections~\ref{sec:data}--\ref{sec:results}): a fail-closed feasibility gate, an
empirical phase diagram that tests the theorem on real calibrated views, and a five-seed benchmark against three
baselines under four attack families. The repository publishes a SHA-256 ledger for every byte of data used.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Byzantine agreement and sensor fusion.} The $n\ge 3k{+}1$ bound for agreement~\cite{pease1980reaching,lamport1982byzantine}
and Marzullo's fault-tolerant interval fusion~\cite{marzullo1990tolerating} assume every sensor measures the same
quantity. In secure state estimation~\cite{fawzi2014secure}, recovering the state requires that the system remain
observable after any $2k$ sensors are removed. Our bounds play the same role for multi-view geometry. A camera sees only
part of the scene, the redundancy that matters is therefore per zone, and the measurement model is projective, so it
has degenerate directions that an adversary can exploit (the epipolar ambiguity).

\textbf{Multi-view geometry and detection.} The epipolar constraint and ground-plane homographies are
classical~\cite{hartley2004multiple}. Multi-camera people detection fuses views through occupancy
maps~\cite{fleuret2008pom} or learned perspective transforms~\cite{hou2020mvdet}, and WILDTRACK~\cite{chavdarova2018wildtrack}
is the standard calibrated benchmark. These methods assume honest views, whereas we use cross-view consistency to
\emph{attest} views.

\textbf{Surveillance and aviation security.} Surveys catalogue CCTV attack surfaces~\cite{costin2016iotcam}
and the absence of authentication in ADS-B~\cite{strohmeier2015security,costin2016ghost}. OT security guidance~\cite{nist80082}
treats availability as primary for operational systems, but it does not quantify how much redundancy keeps a zone
\emph{attestable}.

\textbf{Coverage optimization and ST-GNNs.} Camera placement and stream selection are maximal-covering
problems~\cite{murray1999coverage,horster2006sensor}, and spatio-temporal GNNs~\cite{yu2018stgcn,gilmer2017mpnn} are
standard for sensor graphs. Our MILP differs from these in two ways: trust enters as a constraint, and redundancy for
attestation enters as a second-witness objective.

\section{System and Threat Model}\label{sec:model}
\subsection{Bounded-degree visibility graph}
Let $V=\{1,\dots,n\}$ be cameras with calibrated projections $P_c=K_c[R_c\,|\,t_c]$, and let $\mathcal{Z}$ be a partition
of the monitored ground into zones with criticality weights $w_z\ge 0$, $\sum_z w_z=1$. The coverage set of zone $z$
is $C(z)=\{c: z \text{ projects inside the image of } c\}$, and its \emph{redundancy} is $r(z)=|C(z)|$.
\begin{definition}[Visibility graph]
$G=(V,E)$ with $(i,j)\in E$ iff $C^{-1}(i)\cap C^{-1}(j)\neq\emptyset$ (the two cameras share a zone). Edge weights
count shared zones, and $\Delta(G)$ is the maximum degree.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Site-Class-Aware Security Priority Vector}
\begin{definition}[SCSPV]
For site class $s$, $\boldsymbol{\pi}_s=(\pi_A,\pi_I,\pi_D)$ on the simplex weights availability (coverage-weighted
blind minutes $\mathcal{B}$), integrity (deceived minutes $\mathcal{I}$: zones whose only trusted coverage is a
compromised stream) and disruption ($\mathcal{D}$: false-alarm dispatches plus honest-camera quarantine minutes). The
operating loss is $\mathcal{L}_s=\pi_A\mathcal{B}+\pi_I\mathcal{I}+\pi_D\mathcal{D}$.
\end{definition}
We use $\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\text{airport}}=(0.55,0.30,0.15)$ and, for contrast, $\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\text{nuclear}}=(0.15,0.70,0.15)$.
The vector sets the decision threshold (chosen on validation episodes) and the MILP weights. It is a policy input, not
something learned from data.

\subsection{Adversary}
Up to $k$ cameras are Byzantine during an attack window. The adversary knows all calibrations, and its cameras may
collude. It may: (i) \emph{replay}, re-injecting the camera's own earlier frames through a drone-borne relay that
buffers to hide the added path delay; (ii) \emph{deauthenticate}, causing Gilbert--Elliott burst loss~\cite{gilbert1960capacity}
and re-association spikes; (iii) \emph{saturate RTSP}, producing latency ramps, jitter, freezes and drops; (iv)
\emph{fabricate} analytics metadata, inserting a phantom trajectory that is geometrically consistent across all
colluding views, placed where the fewest honest cameras can see it, while erasing a real person. ADS-B/Remote-ID
inputs are untrusted throughout. Honest cameras report their real observations, which include real occlusions and
annotation noise.

\section{Compression-by-Contradiction Bounds}\label{sec:theory}
Fix a target hypothesis $X$ (a ground point, or a 3-D point for off-plane objects such as heads or low-altitude drones).
Camera $c\in C(X)$ \emph{supports} $X$ if its report is geometrically consistent with $X$. On the ground plane, the
homography $H_c$ is invertible, so an honest report supports exactly one ground point. Off the plane, an honest report
pins down only a ray, and every $X'$ on that ray satisfies the epipolar constraint $p^\top E p'=0$ with the report.
\begin{definition}[Detection and masking]
An attestor \emph{detects} at redundancy $r$ if, for every Byzantine set $B\subseteq C(X)$ with $|B|\le k$ and every
report strategy, any fabricated hypothesis is contradicted by an honest witness. It \emph{masks} if the true hypothesis
strictly out-supports every alternative.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Rigidity of a healthy view]\label{lem:rigid}
If $H_c$ is non-singular, an honest ground-plane report supports exactly one ground hypothesis. For two honest views
$i,j$ with non-parallel rays and baseline $t_{ij}\neq 0$, the pair supports at most one 3-D point.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first claim follows because $H_c^{-1}$ is a bijection of the projective plane. For the second, the back-projected
rays of the two reports intersect in at most one point unless they coincide. Coinciding rays would put both camera
centres on the same line, which contradicts non-parallel rays with $t_{ij}\neq 0$~\cite{hartley2004multiple}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Compression-by-Contradiction]\label{thm:main}
Let $r=|C(X)|$ and $k\ge 1$. On the ground plane, detection is possible iff $r\ge k{+}1$ and masking iff $r\ge 2k{+}1$.
Off the plane, with an adversary free to place fabrications on an honest camera's ray, detection is possible iff
$r\ge k{+}2$ and masking iff $r\ge 2k{+}2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Sufficiency (ground).} Suppose $r\ge k{+}1$. Then at least one honest witness $h$ remains. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid}, $h$ supports only the truth, so it contradicts any fabrication. Suppose $r\ge 2k{+}1$. The truth
has at least $r-k\ge k{+}1$ supporters, while any alternative has at most $k$, the Byzantine ones.

\emph{Sufficiency (off-plane).} A fabrication $X'$ can be supported by at most one honest camera $h_0$, the one whose
ray it lies on. If two honest cameras supported $X'$ and both also supported the truth $X\neq X'$, then
Lemma~\ref{lem:rigid} would be violated. So with $r\ge k{+}2$ at least one honest non-degenerate witness contradicts
$X'$. Suppose $r\ge 2k{+}2$. Then $X$ has at least $r-k\ge k{+}2$ supporters, including $h_0$, while $X'$ has at most
$k{+}1$.

\emph{Necessity: the compression contradiction.} Suppose $r=2k$ on the ground plane. Split $C(X)$ into halves $B_0,B_1$
of size $k$. In world $W_0$ the truth is $X$, and $B_0$ is Byzantine and reports $X'$. In world $W_1$ the truth is $X'$,
and $B_1$ is Byzantine and reports $X$. In both worlds every camera in $B_0$ reports $X'$ and every camera in $B_1$
reports $X$. The report vectors are identical, so no rule can mask. Off the plane with $r=2k{+}1$, use the same
construction on $C(X)\setminus\{h_0\}$, with $h_0$ reporting the shared ray in both worlds. For detection at $r=k$ (or
$k{+}1$ off the plane), let every non-degenerate witness be Byzantine. The fabrication is then supported by every
report, and nothing contradicts it.

The name reflects the following. A trajectory observed by $r$ cameras has $2r$ image measurements for 2 ground degrees
of freedom, a redundant code. To pass a fabricated trajectory as real, the adversary must make the code consistent
with fewer independent witnesses, i.e., \emph{compress} it into its own $k$ views (plus $h_0$ off the plane). The
compression succeeds exactly when the honest remainder is too small to contradict it.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Bounded-degree visibility rigidity]\label{cor:degree}
Every $C(z)$ is a clique in $G$, so $r(z)\le\omega(G)\le\Delta(G)+1$. Ground-plane masking of any zone therefore
requires $k\le\lfloor\Delta/2\rfloor$, and off-plane masking requires $k\le\lfloor(\Delta-1)/2\rfloor$. The minimum
redundancy is $m(n,k)=2k{+}1$ when $2k{+}1\le\min(n,\Delta{+}1)$ and is infeasible otherwise.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Imperfect honest witnesses]\label{prop:prob}
If each honest non-degenerate witness independently contradicts a fabrication with probability $p$, the miss
probability is $(1-p)^{r-k-\mathbb{1}[\text{off}]}$. A target miss rate $\delta$ is therefore met by
$m=k+\mathbb{1}[\text{off}]+\lceil\log\delta/\log(1-p)\rceil$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Blind-time decomposition]\label{prop:blind}
Let a compromised camera be quarantined $L$ seconds after the attack begins. Suppose the hand-off restores coverage to
every zone that keeps at least one honest covering camera within the analytics budget. Then the coverage-weighted
blind time of a window of length $T$ satisfies
$\mathcal{B}\le L\sum_{z:C(z)\cap B\neq\emptyset} w_z + T\sum_{z:C(z)\subseteq B} w_z$ (plus benign outages).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A zone with an honest covering camera stops being blind once hand-off occurs, at most $L$ after onset. A zone covered
only by $B$ is blind for the whole window. Summing the weighted durations gives the bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Availability-first vs.\ integrity-first]
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} explains why the two site classes demand different redundancy. An integrity-first site must
\emph{mask}, which needs $2k{+}1$. An availability-first site only needs to \emph{detect}, which needs $k{+}1$, and then
hand off. On the same camera budget, the airport can therefore tolerate about twice as many colluders per zone.
Proposition~\ref{prop:blind} then makes detection latency $L$ the quantity to minimize.
\end{remark}

\section{Passive-Chain Attestor and Optical Hand-off}\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Cross-view evidence}
At frame $t$, each camera reports detections (foot and head keypoints). For every overlapping pair $(i,j)$, foot points
of $i$ that lie inside $j$'s field of view are mapped to the ground through $H_i^{-1}$ and matched to $j$'s with the
Hungarian algorithm (1\,m gate). This yields the inconsistency rates $\iota_{ij},\iota_{ji}$ (fraction left unmatched)
and the mean ground residual. Matched head points give the symmetric epipolar distance under
$F_{ij}=K_j^{-\top}[t_{ij}]_\times R_{ij}K_i^{-1}$. Each camera also contributes its own temporal evidence:
Horn--Schunck optical flow~\cite{horn1981determining} between consecutive delivered frames (flow magnitude, photometric
residual after warping, raw difference, freeze flag) and transport telemetry (arrival-latency $z$-score against a
warm-up baseline, jitter, gap length).

\subsection{ST-GNN attestor}
Node features $x_c^t\in\mathbb{R}^{15}$ are encoded and passed through a GRU cell, which keeps the model causal. Two
edge-conditioned message-passing layers follow, of the form
$m_{ij}=\phi([h_i,h_j,e_{ij}])\,\sigma(\psi(e_{ij}))\,a_{ij}$ and $h_i\leftarrow\mathrm{LN}(h_i+\rho([h_i,\overline{m}_i]))$,
where $e_{ij}$ holds the pairwise residuals. A linear head outputs the logit $\ell_c^t$. The model has \GnnParams{}
parameters and no physics-informed loss.

\textbf{Bounded untrusted prior.} The ADS-B/Remote-ID activity $a^t$ never enters the encoder. It is added as
$\ell_c^t\mathrel{+}=\beta\tanh(\alpha a^t+b)$, so for any spoofed prior $|\Delta\ell|\le 2\beta$, with $\beta=\SpoofBeta$.
In the MILP the prior modulates zone weights only within $[1,1+\beta]$. Neither channel can create or erase geometric
evidence.

\subsection{Availability-weighted MILP hand-off}
Given risk $\hat p_c$, quarantine flags $q_c$ (hysteresis: on after 2 frames above $\theta$, off after 6 below) and an
analytics budget of $B$ streams, we solve
\begin{align}
\max_{x,y,u}\;& \textstyle\sum_z w_z y_z+\gamma\sum_z w_z u_z-\lambda\sum_c \hat p_c x_c \nonumber\\
\text{s.t. }& y_z\le \textstyle\sum_c a_{cz}(1-q_c)x_c,\quad y_z+u_z\le \textstyle\sum_c a_{cz}(1-q_c)x_c,\nonumber\\
& \textstyle\sum_c x_c\le B,\; x\in\{0,1\}^n,\; y\in\{0,1\}^{|\mathcal Z|},\; u\in[0,1]^{|\mathcal Z|},\label{eq:milp}
\end{align}
with $\gamma=0.25$ and $\lambda=0.02$, using SciPy's HiGHS interface~\cite{huangfu2018highs}. Zones with identical
coverage signatures are merged and their weights summed. This is exact because their constraints coincide, and it
shrinks WILDTRACK from 432 zones to 26.

\section{Data, Gate~0 and Protocol}\label{sec:data}
\textbf{Real data only.} (i) WILDTRACK~\cite{chavdarova2018wildtrack}: seven synchronized, calibrated, overlapping
1920$\times$1080 cameras with 400 annotated frames at 2\,fps. The 6.8\,GB archive was read by HTTP range requests,
including a repair of 32-bit-wrapped ZIP offsets, and each of the 2,800 frames was SHA-256 hashed before 4$\times$
down-sampling. (ii) OpenSky Network~\cite{schafer2014opensky}: 90 snapshots of live ADS-B state vectors at 10\,s
intervals around JFK, ATL and DCA, each raw response hashed. (iii) OpenStreetMap~\cite{osm}: aeroway runway, taxiway,
apron, terminal and stand geometry for the same airports. All hashes, URLs and licences are in
\texttt{artifacts/data\_provenance.json}. No frames, detections or aircraft coordinates were synthesized.

\textbf{What is emulated, and how.} Attacks re-route real content (a camera's own past frames, freezes, drops) or tamper
with metadata (Section~\ref{sec:model}). WILDTRACK carries no network telemetry, so arrival latency and jitter follow
standard channel models: Gaussian jitter with per-camera baselines, Gilbert--Elliott loss~\cite{gilbert1960capacity},
and a saturation ramp. Benign congestion episodes on honest cameras, which occur in 35\% of episodes, create
false-alarm pressure. ADS-B spoofing is emulated by \emph{transplanting} one airport's real traffic onto another, so no
coordinates are invented. Detections are the dataset's annotated boxes. This isolates attestation from detector error
but keeps real occlusion and labelling noise.

\textbf{Gate~0.} Before any experiment, a fail-closed gate checks four things, and the pipeline returns
\texttt{NOT\_EVALUABLE} on any failure. (a) All homographies are invertible. (b) Annotated ground positions re-project
onto the box foot points, with median error between \GzeroReprojMin{} and \GzeroReprojMax{}\,px across cameras
(\GzeroBoxes{} boxes). (c) Cross-view homography transfer of the same person has median error \GzeroTransferMed{}\,cm
and 90th percentile \GzeroTransferPninety{}\,cm (\GzeroPairs{} pairs). (d) The visibility graph is connected, with
$\Delta=\GzeroMaxDegree$. The gate passed.

\textbf{Protocol.} Seeds $\{42,101,202,303,404\}$. Camera subsets $n'\in\{5,6,7\}$ with $k\in\{1,2,3\}$ give
$k/n'\in\{0.14,0.17,0.20,0.29,0.33,0.40,0.43\}$. Training and validation episodes use frames 0--199, and test episodes
use the temporally disjoint frames 200--399: 7 configurations $\times$ 5 attack types $\times$ 3 episodes per seed. The
analytics budget is $B=\lceil 0.4n'\rceil$. Thresholds minimize the airport SCSPV loss on validation episodes. The
baselines are: (1) \emph{single-camera analytics}, which uses own-stream telemetry and flow with greedy hand-off; (2)
\emph{naive majority vote}, which flags a camera if most overlapping neighbours disagree, with greedy hand-off; and (3)
\emph{static redundancy}, a pre-provisioned two-witness set with majority voting inside it and no runtime hand-off.

\section{Limitations}
Detections are annotated boxes rather than detector outputs, so the measured benefit concerns attestation, not
detection. Network telemetry is modelled, not recorded, and the airport case study uses a hypothetical camera layout
because real layouts are not public. The ADS-B window is a single overnight capture, with sparse traffic and no DCA
movements during its curfew, so the prior's benefit is under-tested by design. Finally, WILDTRACK is a 12$\times$36\,m
plaza with seven cameras. The bounds in Section~\ref{sec:theory} do not depend on scale, but the learned attestor's
numbers should be re-measured on larger deployments.

\section{Conclusion}
Availability-first airport surveillance needs a principled answer to how much redundancy is enough. Each zone needs
$k{+}1$ healthy witnesses to \emph{detect} collusion ($k{+}2$ off the ground plane), while masking needs $2k{+}1$
($2k{+}2$), and a bounded-degree visibility graph caps both. On real calibrated views, the theorem's phase boundary
appears where predicted for ground targets and one witness later for crowded off-plane targets, as the probabilistic
extension anticipates. Combining a geometry-aware ST-GNN with an availability-weighted MILP turns the theory into a
controller whose untrusted inputs have provably bounded influence.
